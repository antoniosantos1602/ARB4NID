Key Features

Tool Usage

The ARB4NID was designed to be used by individuals without requiring prior knowledge of the adversarial domain. To operate it, the user only needs to provide pre-processed data by uploading the test datasets, along with the corresponding model stored in joblib format and its associated scaler. Additionally, the target feature (label) must be provided as one-hot encoded labels.

Multiple Attacks

Attacks are categorized by the attacker’s level of knowledge about the target model:

  • White-Box — full access to the model’s internals; uses gradient calculations to craft precise perturbations. Only applicable to differentiable models
  • Gray-Box — partial knowledge of the model; may leverage architecture details without full gradient access.
  • Black-Box — no access to model internals; relies solely on input-output queries, making it applicable to any model type.

Attacks can also be configured by objective:

  • Targeted — forces the model to predict a specific predefined class.
  • Untargeted — simply causes a misclassification into any class other than the original.

Adversarial Metrics
  • Adversarial Accuracy (AA) — Percentage of correctly classified samples for which the model maintained the correct prediction after the adversarial perturbations were applied.
  • Attack Success Rate (ASR) — Percentage of correctly classified samples that were induced into an incorrect classification by the adversarial attack.
  • Realistic Adversarial Accuracy (RAA) — Measure of model robustness that jointly accounts for resistance to adversarial attacks and the realism of the generated perturbations, penalising examples that violate domain constraints proportionally to the degree of violation.
  • Attack Reputation Score (ARS) — Adversarial success rate weighted by the reputation of each adversarial sample, reflecting the proportion of examples that fool the model while preserving coherence with the semantic, logical and statistical constraints of the domain.
Robustness Evaluation

ARB4NID measures model robustness through the Realistic Adversarial Accuracy (RAA), an accuracy-based metric that reflects the proportion of correctly classified samples a model preserves when exposed to adversarial examples that are both successful and realistic. Higher RAA values correspond to higher robustness.

Because an attacker only needs one effective strategy while a defender must resist them all, robustness is characterized jointly through two complementary indicators:

  • RAA Mean — the average RAA across all evaluated attacks, providing an overall view of the model's behaviour.
  • RAA Min — the lowest RAA among the evaluated attacks, identifying the worst-case attack and exposing specific weaknesses that the average alone may hide.

Each attack's RAA is mapped to a robustness level:

RAA (%) Robustness Level
90 – 100Very High
70 – 90High
50 – 70Moderate
< 50Low

By combining RAA Mean and RAA Min, ARB4NID classifies each model into one of four robustness profiles — using 70% (the Moderate→High boundary) as the reference for the mean and 50% (the Low→Moderate boundary) for the minimum:

Highly Robust — Mean ≥ 70% and Min ≥ 50%: consistently robust across all attacks, with no critical weakness under any specific method.
Robust but Vulnerable — Mean ≥ 70% but Min < 50%: strong on average, but a specific attack drives its robustness down to a low level.
Moderately Robust — Mean < 70% but Min ≥ 50%: no critical weakness, yet with clear room for improvement across all attacks.
Low Robustness — Mean < 70% and Min < 50%: limited overall and significantly affected by at least one attack strategy.
